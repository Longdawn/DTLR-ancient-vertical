\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Scene Text and Line-Level Recognition}

Text recognition converts cropped text images into character sequences and is central to OCR, scene text understanding, handwriting recognition, and historical document digitization. Earlier systems often relied on explicit character segmentation or localization before classification and sequence post-processing. Such pipelines are interpretable, but they are sensitive to segmentation errors under touching characters, deformed strokes, and degraded images, and they require costly character-level annotations.

Deep learning has made segmentation-free recognition the dominant paradigm. CRNN integrates convolutional features, recurrent sequence modeling, and CTC into an end-to-end recognizer~\cite{shi2017crnn}. Later work improved recognition under irregular geometry, context modeling, and evaluation protocols: RARE and ASTER rectify irregular text images~\cite{shi2016rare,shi2018aster}; Baek et al. analyze how training and testing data affect fair comparisons~\cite{baek2019wrong}; ABINet and PARSeq introduce explicit or internal language modeling~\cite{fang2021abinet,bautista2022parseq}; SVTR models local and global visual dependencies with a single visual model~\cite{du2022svtr}. These recognizers provide natural baselines for vertical ancient-text recognition. However, scene text typically emphasizes perspective, curved text, background, and illumination, whereas ancient books more often contain paper degradation, ink diffusion, broken strokes, historical glyphs, and charset mismatch. For vertical ancient columns, character-level spatial organization is a useful training signal rather than a nuisance factor.

\subsection{Detection-Style Recognition and Query Models}

Detection-style recognition treats a text line as a set of character instances: the model predicts character categories and positions, and a transcription is obtained by ordering the instances. This formulation can explicitly use character positions, but a full detection-recognition pipeline may suffer from thresholding, instance filtering, sorting, and error propagation. Query-based object detectors such as DETR and DINO provide a flexible set-prediction mechanism for object localization~\cite{carion2020detr,zhang2023dino}, and recent detection-based text line recognition explores related ideas for line recognition~\cite{baena2024dtlr}.

SAQT differs from inference-time detection-recognition pipelines. It does not output detected characters as the final recognition result. Character boxes supervise the queries only during character-localization information learning. The downstream recognizer sorts query outputs and trains with CTC using line-level transcriptions. This design keeps the final interface as sequence recognition while retaining the spatial benefit of character-level supervision.

\subsection{Chinese and Historical Document Recognition}

Chinese recognition is challenging because the charset is large, glyph structures are complex, and character frequencies are highly imbalanced. Many characters share visually similar components, and low-frequency or unseen characters can strongly affect recognition. Existing work has used radical-level, stroke-level, or structural decomposition to address large-vocabulary and zero-shot Chinese character recognition~\cite{diao2023rzcr,zeng2022star}.

Ancient and historical document recognition adds visual degradation and collection-specific character usage to these difficulties. Historical document analysis surveys connect recognition with datasets, document structure, and degradation modeling~\cite{lombardi2020historicalsurvey}; historical book recognition benchmarks further emphasize the variability of real scanned books~\cite{antonacopoulos2013hbr}. For Chinese historical documents, prior work on character segmentation, character detection, and vertical organization shows that spatial structure is not incidental background~\cite{tang2020hrcenternet,wu2020precise,ma2024reading}. SAQT focuses on cropped single-column recognition: it uses character boxes as localization supervision during training, while the final model is trained with line-level transcriptions and outputs a character sequence.

\subsection{CTC Decoding and Metrics}

CTC marginalizes over all paths that collapse to the target sequence and therefore trains sequence models without explicit alignments~\cite{graves2006ctc}. Greedy decoding selects the most likely token at each time step, merges repeated tokens, and removes blank tokens. The relative confidence between blank and nonblank tokens can affect deletion and insertion behavior. We report accuracy rate (AR) and correct rate (CR). AR penalizes insertion, deletion, and substitution errors, while CR ignores insertions and reflects the retained correct characters.
